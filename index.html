Why most covers underperform

A cover doesn't have to be beautiful.
It has to win a 200-pixel fight.

Your reader meets your cover at thumbnail size, in a grid, beside fifteen competitors, for about a second. Most of what makes a cover “nice” is invisible at that size — and most of what makes it sell is decided long before anyone opens Photoshop.

Our framework

Every cover is scored before you ever see it.

“It looks nice” is not a standard. Every concept we put in front of you has been through the same six-point review — the one we developed from studying what actually sells in KDP categories, and the reason you're choosing between good options instead of sorting the good from the broken.

01Thumbnail readabilityCan the title be read at the size Amazon actually shows it? This is where most covers quietly fail.
02Visual hierarchyDoes the eye land on the right thing first, second, third — or is everything shouting at once?
03Imagery, concept & storyDoes the picture tell this book's story, or is it decoration that could sit on any book?
04Genre signallingDoes it look like the category it competes in? Break the genre's rules and browsers scroll past without knowing why.
05Composition & spacingMargins, balance, breathing room. The difference between designed and assembled.
06Technical correctnessResolution, bleed, trim, spine, safe zones — everything that decides whether KDP accepts it and print matches screen.

The grayscale test

We strip the colour out. If the cover stops working, it was leaning on colour to do a job that composition and contrast should be doing — and it will fall apart next to a brighter competitor.

The blur / squint test

We blur it until only the strongest shapes survive. Whatever is still legible is what a scrolling buyer actually registers in the second before they decide. If nothing survives, neither does the sale.

And we put it on the shelf beside your competition.

Your cover doesn't get judged on its own. It gets judged in a grid, at thumbnail size, next to the current bestsellers in your category. So that's exactly how we review it — your cover dropped into a simulated Amazon results row beside the books it will actually compete with.

If it doesn't stand out here, it doesn't matter how good it looks at full size.

Questions

Before you order.

How is this different from generating a cover myself?

Three ways. A KDP expert researches your market and competitors first, so the concepts start from what already sells in your category rather than from a prompt. You see dozens of directions and choose, instead of accepting whatever one generation produced. And the result is built manually in fully editable layers, in every format your book needs — not a flattened image you can never change.

What does “unlimited revisions” actually cover?

Once you've picked a direction, we refine it as many times as it takes — typography, colour, imagery, hierarchy — until you're happy. There's no round counter and no upcharge. Deciding you want a completely different direction after we've started building is a conversation rather than an automatic no; we'll tell you honestly what it means for your timeline.

Which formats do I get?

All of them: Kindle, paperback, hardcover and ACX audiobook. Each is a different trim and aspect ratio, and each is built properly rather than stretched from the last one — with the spine calculated to your exact page count. You also get the layered source files, so the design is yours outright.

Do you use AI?

In the exploration stage, yes, and we're open about that — it's why you get dozens of directions instead of three. But nothing ships as a raw AI output. The direction you choose is rebuilt by hand in Photoshop: typography reset properly, artwork cleaned and corrected, every element separated into real layers. If a concept can't be brought to a professional standard, we tell you rather than shipping it.

How do you know a cover will actually work?

We don't guess and we don't put it to a vote in the office. Every concept goes through our six-point review — thumbnail readability, visual hierarchy, imagery and story, genre signalling, composition and spacing, technical correctness — plus the grayscale test and the blur test, which between them tell you whether a cover is carrying its weight or leaning on colour and detail that vanish at browse size. Then we drop it into a simulated Amazon results row beside your actual competitors, because that is the only context in which your buyer will ever see it.

How long does it take?

Concepts typically reach you within a few days of the brief, and the finished files follow once you've chosen a direction and we've worked through revisions. Pace depends mostly on how quickly your feedback comes back — your project manager gives you a schedule for your specific project when it starts.

What if I don't like any of the concepts?

It's rare across dozens of directions, but it happens — and it's usually a sign the brief needs sharpening rather than the design. We'll talk it through and go again on a new direction.

Can you match an existing series?

Yes, and this is where the layered files earn their keep. Send the existing covers and we'll build to the series' typography, palette and layout so the new title sits correctly alongside them on your author page.

Who's actually doing the work?

A small fixed team, not a marketplace. You get a dedicated professional graphic designer who produces every concept for your book and does all the Photoshop finishing by hand — the same person start to finish, not whoever a marketplace assigns this week. And a dedicated project manager who is a working KDP publisher and coaches other publishers; they handle the research, your review page and your KDP upload. Josiah, who founded KDPChimp, has published 100+ books across eight years on KDP.

Why only 10 projects a month?

Because the same designer builds every cover and the same project manager runs every project personally. 10 is what the two of them can do without the quality slipping or your emails going unanswered. When the month is full, we'll tell you the next start date rather than take your money and put you in a queue.
